T480

The ThinkPad you can rebuild from the bones up, and eight years later people still do.

Forget the keyboard, everyone knows. The T480 is a hobby. Graft on a seven-row keyboard, swap in a 4K panel, flash firmware you can read, undervolt it until it runs cool, then put Linux on it like it was born there. Reliable today? Lenovo shipped BIOS 1.56 in October 2025, and modders were still taking BIOS requests in 2026.

Memory and storage

Slots you can actually use

  • RAM: two DDR4-2400 SO-DIMM slots, 8 GB typical at launch.
  • Official max: 32 GB, according to Crucial's compatibility page.
  • User max: 64 GB. A forum poster has run 2 x 32 GB for months without trouble.
  • Storage: M.2 NVMe, officially up to 1 TB.
  • Second drive: the WWAN slot takes a 2242 NVMe with a mod. A Linux user's TLP dump shows two NVMe drives in one T480.

Exhibit C · Coreboot

Run it on firmware you can read.

The T480 is a supported target for both coreboot and Libreboot, which builds on coreboot. It is also one of the more popular ways to get a modern-ish ThinkPad without the vendor firmware blob soup. It is not a weekend toy: the first flash needs a clip on the chip.

16 MiB chip · idle
One block per MiB of the W25Q128. An illustration of erase, write, verify, not a real flash.
  1. Update the stock firmware first. Older Thunderbolt 3 firmware writes logs to its own 4 MB flash chip. When that fills up, the laptop stops booting. Lenovo's update fixes it, and Libreboot and coreboot both say to do this before anything else.
  2. Power down properly. Remove the back cover and unplug the internal battery connector and the CMOS battery. The external battery comes off too.
  3. Clip onto the chip and dump it. The system flash is a 16 MiB SOIC-8 chip. Read it with an external programmer such as a CH341A or a Raspberry Pi. Libreboot wants a dump before the Lenovo update and another before you flash, both verified.
  4. Deal with Boot Guard and the Intel ME. Boot Guard blocks firmware Lenovo didn't sign, so a "deguard" tool bypasses it. A donor ME image comes from a Dell updater, and Libreboot then disables the ME with me_cleaner.
  5. First flash is external. Updates can be internal. After the initial clip-on flash, coreboot's docs say later updates can go in through software, writing only the BIOS region.
Brick warningLibreboot's own page says leaving out the ME and FSP blobs in a release ROM can brick the machine. The Thunderbolt flash is a separate, smaller chip, and flashprog will report a size mismatch if you clip the wrong one.

Small print ThinkPad users care about: Hyper-Threading is off by default in Libreboot, and there is a reversible option to turn it on. The MAC address is identical across release ROMs until you change it with nvmutil (pass --platform sklkbl to ifdtool if you do). EC UART debugging works on the T480 only, not the T480s, and needs the 1.22 vendor EC firmware (N24HT37W), so you downgrade to a vendor BIOS between 1.39 and 1.54 first. Libreboot also notes Thunderbolt broke S3 resume for some people, and a second NVMe works with coreboot only on boards without the discrete GPU.

Exhibit D · The dual heatpipe mod

Swap in the big heatsink. Stop throttling.

Integrated-graphics T480s ship with a small single-pipe heatsink, and plenty of users watch them hit the thermal wall. The fix that keeps coming up is the heavier dual-pipe sink from the dGPU boards.

Single pipe, 69 g96 °C
Reached almost immediately with regular paste, then the CPU throttled.
  1. Find the dual-pipe part. The Habr author bought a 93 g sink with two heatpipes and a dGPU area from AliExpress for $26.
  2. Mask the dGPU area. There's nothing under it on an integrated board, but he covered the soldered area with Kapton tape. The modding guide says to cover it too, so nothing shorts.
  3. Re-paste with care. Fresh MX4 on the small sink helped without stopping the throttling. The big sink with plain paste stopped it. A PTM7950 pad took off a couple more degrees: $8 for a 40 x 80 mm sheet, when the die is about 10 x 19 mm.
  4. Skip liquid metal. He tested it and advised against it in a laptop because of the damage risk.

check what the stock paste job touched. the teardown found paste on the PCH chip, which doesn't need cooling.

Stock single-pipe heatsink69 g
1 copper pipe
An i5-8350U hit about 96 °C almost immediately and throttled. A friend's identical laptop did the same.
Dual-pipe heatsink with the GPU area93 g

Throttling disappeared completely, even with regular paste.

Bar length is heatsink mass, from the Habr teardown, which measured a couple of degrees of gain. The modding guide goes bigger, claiming 15 to 35 °C lower under sustained turbo.

Linux: intel-undervolt
# /etc/intel-undervolt.conf # reported-stable on one T480s. start at -60 on yours. undervolt 0 'CPU' -105 undervolt 1 'GPU' -80 undervolt 2 'CPU Cache' -105 undervolt 3 'System Agent' -80 undervolt 4 'Analog I/O' 0 $ sudo intel-undervolt apply $ sudo systemctl enable --now intel-undervolt # the python tool does the same job: $ sudo undervolt --core -105 --cache -105 --gpu -80 $ sudo undervolt --read
  1. Windows: ThrottleStop. Open FIVR, tick Unlock Adjustable Voltage, and set the same negative offset for CPU Core and CPU Cache. Core alone had no effect for the TechPowerUp poster. Then the iGPU and unslice values, and apply.
  2. Stress it. Loop Cinebench R20, run RealBench or Prime95. The reports above used all three.
  3. Step by 10 mV. Go deeper until it crashes or throws errors, then back off 10–20 mV. A crash from too much undervolt is a hard freeze, so save your work first.
  4. Make it stick. Start ThrottleStop or intel-undervolt at boot. One poster's XTU offsets reverted after reboots.

Undervolt guide for Linux

intel-undervolt works on Haswell and newer Intel CPUs, so the T480's 8th-gen chips are covered. Pick your family.

  1. Install it. It's in the official repos (Arch, EndeavourOS, Manjaro and friends). 
    sudo pacman -S intel-undervolt
  2. Check the kernel lets you. With Secure Boot on, kernel lockdown blocks the register access. You want [none]. If you see [integrity], turn Secure Boot off in the BIOS. 
    cat /sys/kernel/security/lockdown
  3. Look at the current values. 
    sudo intel-undervolt read
  4. Write a gentle config. Edit /etc/intel-undervolt.conf. Keep CPU and CPU Cache at the same value. 
    undervolt 0 'CPU' -60
undervolt 1 'GPU' -40
undervolt 2 'CPU Cache' -60
undervolt 3 'System Agent' -40
undervolt 4 'Analog I/O' 0
  5. Apply and confirm. 
    sudo intel-undervolt apply
sudo intel-undervolt read
  6. Stress it. Run a load and watch the clocks. A bad undervolt shows up as a freeze or a reboot. 
    sudo pacman -S stress-ng
stress-ng --cpu 0 --cpu-method matrixprod --timeout 15m
watch -n1 "grep MHz /proc/cpuinfo"
  7. Step down by 10 mV. Edit, apply, stress again. Stop 20 mV short of the first crash. The reports above cluster at −100 to −125 on core and cache.
  8. Make it stick. 
    sudo systemctl enable --now intel-undervolt
    If the values drop after suspend, add the daemon service too: 
    sudo systemctl enable --now intel-undervolt-loop

Check first

The BIOS may have locked it

What you see
You apply −50 mV and reading it back shows 0, or nothing changes.
Why
Plundervolt mitigations arrived in ThinkPad BIOS updates around late 2019 (an X280 thread cites version 1.30). T480 owners on BIOS 1.50 and 1.52 have asked modders for a BIOS that unhides the Advanced menu, which one said they needed for undervolting.
What people tried
Downgrading (an X280 owner's flash failed with "secureflash authentication failed"), or a per-machine modded BIOS built from your own dump. Both carry risk. See the BIOS-mod card in the wild section.

Sources: ThinkPads forum X280 thread · bios-mods.com T480 thread

LinuxSoftware fix

The tool does nothing on Linux

What you see
intel-undervolt or throttled runs but offsets don't apply, or it errors on MSR access.
Why
With Secure Boot on, the kernel enables Lockdown, which blocks the register access these tools need. Thermald can also fight with them.
The fix
Turn Secure Boot off (or disable the Lockdown LSM), and disable thermald or check that it isn't overriding your limits.

Source: dipch's throttling-fix README

BIOS patched by a stranger

Modders on forums unlock the hidden Advanced menu and strip the Wi-Fi/WWAN whitelist from T480 BIOS images. They need your dump, read with a CH341A clip and ASProgrammer, because the mod is per-machine. Exhibit I · Linux

It runs Linux like it was born there.

Every ThinkPad user eventually puts a penguin on it. The T480 is the generation where almost everything works out of the box, and the few things that don't have a known fix. Here is what the forums keep telling each other.

~ on a T480, as one does
$ ls /sys/class/power_supply AC BAT0 BAT1 # two batteries, both visible. the kernel sees both. $ sudo tlp setcharge 75 80 BAT0 $ sudo tlp setcharge 75 80 BAT1 Setting temporary charge thresholds for BAT0: start = 75, stop = 80 # battery care, per pack, no vendor app needed $ sudo fwupdmgr update Devices with no available firmware updates: none # BIOS and Thunderbolt firmware through LVFS $ cat /proc/acpi/ibm/fan | head -3 status: enabled speed: 2310 level: auto # thinkpad_acpi, in-tree, no extra driver
BAT0 and BAT1 are separate

Charge thresholds are set per battery, so you can keep the internal cell and the rear brick at different limits. TLP and the kernel's thinkpad_acpi handle it.

Sleep state option in the BIOS

Newer ThinkPads let you pick a Linux sleep mode so suspend behaves, a setting users bring up whenever someone asks why the battery drains overnight.

The throttling daemon

The 8th gen U-series chips get power-limit clamped by firmware. Linux users run throttled or thermald to lift it, and pair it with the dual heatpipe mod above.

TrackPoint under libinput

The red nub works with no setup, and you can tune the sensitivity and speed through udev rules or your desktop's settings.

One cable, Thunderbolt 3

bolt authorizes docks on Linux, so the one-cable desk setup carries over.

Firmware updates without Windows

fwupd pulls BIOS and Thunderbolt updates from LVFS, so nobody has to dig up a Windows installer.

Exhibit J · The catch

Every known problem. And its fix.

It's not perfect. Nothing 2018 is. Here is each thing ThinkPad users actually run into, what causes it, and what people did about it. Green means a software fix, orange means you'll need a screwdriver, red means the threads never settled it.

PerformanceSoftware fix

It throttles long before it's hot

What you see
Clocks sag under load and the CPU sits at a low wattage, sometimes while the chassis is barely warm. One thread's user was throttling below 60 °C.
Why
Forum experts blamed Lenovo's firmware: a high PROCHOT offset that trips the CPU's thermal brake far below its real limit (one wrote about throttling near 65 °C), plus power limits that get lowered on the fly. On Linux, Ubuntu's bug tracker confirms PL1/PL2 come up wrong, and it persisted on BIOS 1.19.
The fix
Windows: in ThrottleStop, turn on PROCHOT Offset, set it to 3 (some users pick 10) and tick Lock. Lock the turbo power limits with the MMIO lock option in the TPL window. If you undervolt, apply the same offset to core and cache: the thread's author saw no effect from core alone (−100 mV was their number). Start it from Task Scheduler.
Linux: run throttled, a Python script that rewrites the power registers. It doesn't work with Secure Boot on.

Sources: TechPowerUp thread · Ubuntu bug 1815214

PortsHardware fix

Thunderbolt and USB-C stop working

What you see
USB-C or Thunderbolt dead, docks not detected, no HDMI over USB-C, charging capped around 15 W or gone, a Thunderbolt error at POST. Threads about exactly this exist for the T480.
Why
Lenovo said old Thunderbolt 3 firmware wears out the SPI-ROM chip that stores the controller's own firmware. T480 owners describe the same symptoms on the forums.
The fix
Install Lenovo's Thunderbolt firmware patch (released August 2019, delivered through Lenovo Vantage) before anything else. Don't force-update a controller that's already misbehaving. If the chip is already worn, the repairs people describe are reflashing it with a clip or replacing the Winbond SPI chip and flashing it. If you're buying used, plug a dock in before you pay.

Sources: Notebookcheck, Thunderbolt 3 failure · ThinkPads forum

PortsEasy fix

The dock or monitor gets no signal

What you see
A USB-C dock or display works in one port and does nothing in the other.
Why
Only one of the T480's two USB-C ports supports USB 3.1 Gen 2, Power Delivery, DisplayPort and Thunderbolt 3.
The fix
Move the cable to the other USB-C port. Use that same port when you update the Thunderbolt firmware.

Source: CloudsPress T480 upgrade guide

ScreenCheck before buying

Some panels flicker

What you see
Eye strain or headaches on some configurations. Notebookcheck flagged specific T480 panels as a risk for anyone sensitive to flicker.
Why
PWM backlight dimming on certain panels. The FrankenPad builder described a high-pitched noise from the stock FHD panel as well.
The fix
Read the panel part number in the listing before you buy. Or swap it: the T480's display connector has four lanes where older ThinkPads have two, which is why QHD and 4K panels work. The FrankenPad builder fitted a UHD panel where the PWM frequency could be raised without the whine.

Sources: Notebookcheck review · kitsunyan's FrankenPad write-up

InputSoftware fix

TrackPoint dies after suspend

What you see
After waking from sleep, the TrackPoint and touchpad don't respond until you cold boot.
Why
Reported on a T480 board running in a modified T25 chassis, so it may be specific to that build. Worth knowing about if you transplant parts.
The fix
That builder found that leaving NFC enabled in the BIOS avoided it. A cold boot is the fallback.

Source: kitsunyan's FrankenPad write-up

HeatHardware fix

The chassis runs hot

What you see
Fans spin up, the palmrest warms and the i7 won't hold its boost clocks. The ThrottleStop thread's author called the chassis hot and found fan-curve tweaks only partly helped.
Why
A small heatsink and a slim chassis, plus the firmware limits above.
The fix
Fresh paste first. Then the tricks from Exhibit E: the dual heatpipe heatsink, a PTM7950 pad, and liquid metal if you accept the risk. The FrankenPad builder measured an i7-8650U holding about 3.4–3.5 GHz in a 15-minute stress test after liquid metal and an undervolt. T480s users also report copper pads. See the cooling exhibit.

Sources: TechPowerUp thread · FrankenPad write-up · Habr

More fixes, by operating system

LinuxLaggy or jumpy touchpad and TrackPoint

Create /etc/modprobe.d/psmouse.conf containing options psmouse synaptics_intertouch=1, then reload the psmouse module.

LinuxThunderbolt dock won't authorize

In the BIOS set Security → Thunderbolt 3 to User Authorization. Update the controller from Linux with fwupdmgr refresh, get-updates, then update.

LinuxSudden shutdown when swapping the rear battery

The system may not switch packs cleanly. A GitHub project, battery_swap_fix_t480, provides fix scripts.

LinuxFingerprint reader

Needs kernel 5.8.1 or newer, plus libfprint-tod and python-validity.

LinuxCellular modem dead

The same guide says the Fibocom L850-GL had no working Linux driver. Its suggestions: a USB modem or a different WWAN card.

LinuxWi-Fi missing with a Broadcom card

Intel cards work out of the box. For Broadcom, find the card with lspci -nn -d 14e4: and install bcmwl-kernel-source.

LinuxNVIDIA MX150 models

They need the proprietary driver: sudo ubuntu-drivers autoinstall on Ubuntu.

LinuxBattery thresholds, fan curves

The usual answers are TLP for charge thresholds and thinkfan for fan control. Pick TLP or power-profiles-daemon, not both.

WindowsUndervolt resets after reboot

Run ThrottleStop at logon from Task Scheduler with highest privileges, with the apply-voltages-at-start options ticked. One Tom's Guide poster's XTU offsets reverted after reboots.

WindowsThunderbolt firmware

Get the controller firmware patch through Lenovo's update tools before docks or USB-C start misbehaving.

BothBattery drains while shut down

Reported on a T480s. The suggested fix was a BIOS option that turns off USB charging when the system is off. If it keeps happening, contact Lenovo.

All of these are individual reports from forums and reviews. Check your own BIOS version and panel before you try anything, and back up before flashing.

Exhibit K · Power Bridge

Two batteries. One keeps you alive while you swap the other.

A small battery sits inside. A bigger one clips onto the back. Reviewers call out that the T480 is a rare modern laptop with a removable battery plus an internal one. Pick a build, then hot-swap it.

24 Wh inside
24 Wh rear
48Wh total on your back

00:00uptime, never reset
Running on BAT0 + BAT1.

An illustration of the idea, not a measurement.

  1. Rear battery getting low? Keep working. Nothing special to do.
  2. Slide the latch and pull it off. The internal cell carries the laptop. One user on a forum tried it for the first time just to check and the machine never went dark.
  3. Click a fresh one in. Charged spares are the whole trick. People carry two.
  4. Or skip batteries entirely. The charge port is USB-C, so a fast enough power bank works as a fourth option.

fine print I love: if the internal one is already flat, the swap trick doesn't save you. Check before you pull.

Linux users have notes too. There's an Arch forum thread on hot-switching between the two batteries, and a FreeBSD user whose machine crashed when battery 0 hit 0% while battery 1 still read 100%. The two packs don't always drain the way you'd guess.

Verdict

Put Linux on it. Then never look back.

Eight years old, and the whole Linux world is still pointed at it. BIOS and Thunderbolt firmware update from the command line through fwupd. TLP and thinkfan handle power and fans. throttled and intel-undervolt take the heat out. There is a coreboot and Libreboot port, a Qubes thread that never ends, even OpenCore builds. The T480 is the age where the drivers are boring and the guides are long. The rough edges are short: the cellular modem and the fingerprint reader.

So buy a clean one, drop in 32 GB, swap the screen if you like, run the undervolt guide above, and install Arch or Ubuntu. That's the whole pitch.

— an intensive ThinkPad user
